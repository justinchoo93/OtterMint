About these numbers
  • Everything is in today's dollars. Returns are after inflation and move in a straight line; there are no bad years, which is why the return after retiring is set lower. The lower return starts when the first of you stops working.
  • Until someone retires, saving stays flat: the brokerage gets the cash you saved over the last 12 months, the 401(k) and 457(b) get the amounts in the table, PERS 3 gets 10% of salary. No raises. While only one of you works, that person keeps contributing, their take-home pay covers spending first, and any surplus goes to the brokerage.
  • Spending is flat apart from the changes you enter, which start once one of you stops working. Health insurance is free while either of you works, then half the amount for each of you until you each turn 65. There are no Medicare premiums and no one-off costs like a car, house or college.
  • One tax rate applies to every withdrawal, including Roth and brokerage money that would really be taxed less. The pension and Social Security are treated as tax-free.
  • Withdrawals go brokerage, crypto, 457(b), PERS 3 investment account, 401(k), then Roth IRAs, taking only from what is open. A 401(k) or PERS 3 investment account opens if its owner stops working in or after the year they turn 55, otherwise at 59½; for PERS 3 that follows the IRS rule, though DRS only says the penalty "might" apply. The 457(b) opens when your partner leaves. Roth contributions come out any time, growth at 59½. No Roth conversions, 72(t) payments or required minimum distributions.
  • The PERS 3 pension is 1% of salary per year of service, using today's salary as the final average. It is reduced by DRS's factors when it starts before 65; ages between DRS's published ones are interpolated. After your partner leaves it only grows with 20 or more years of service, so inflation wears it down until it starts. Once it starts it keeps pace with inflation (DRS caps raises at 3% a year) and is paid as a single life benefit; a survivor option would lower it.
  • Social Security uses SSA's rules: 30% less at 62, 24% more at 70. Retiring early also lowers the benefit itself, so use your ssa.gov estimate.
  • Cash and home equity aren't counted. A date works if the money lasts to the plan age with anything left. The search stops at 70.
